# Motion Along a Straight Line

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 3
> ← [[Vectors]] · Next: [[Motion in Two and Three Dimensions]] →

**Motion Along a Straight Line** is one-dimensional **kinematics**: describing motion (position, velocity, acceleration) without asking what causes it.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Displacement:** $\displaystyle \Delta x = x_f - x_i$
> - **Average velocity:** $\displaystyle \bar{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
> - **Instantaneous velocity:** $\displaystyle v(t) = \frac{dx(t)}{dt}$
> - **Average speed:** $\displaystyle \bar{s} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Elapsed time}}$
> - **Average acceleration:** $\displaystyle \bar{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_0}{t_f - t_0}$
> - **Instantaneous acceleration:** $\displaystyle a(t) = \frac{dv(t)}{dt}$
> - **Average velocity (constant a):** $\displaystyle \bar{v} = \frac{v_0 + v}{2}$
> - **Velocity from acceleration:** $\displaystyle v = v_0 + at$
> - **Position from velocity and acceleration:** $\displaystyle x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
> - **Velocity from distance:** $\displaystyle v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$
> - **Velocity of free fall:** $\displaystyle v = v_0 - gt$
> - **Height of free fall:** $\displaystyle y = y_0 + v_0 t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
> - **Velocity of free fall from height:** $\displaystyle v^2 = v_0^2 - 2g(y - y_0)$
> - **Velocity by integration:** $\displaystyle v(t) = \int a(t)\,dt + C_1$
> - **Position by integration:** $\displaystyle x(t) = \int v(t)\,dt + C_2$

## Position, Displacement, and Average Velocity
- **Displacement** is the change in position — it has magnitude and direction (SI unit: meter).
- **Distance traveled** is the total path length.
- **Average velocity** is displacement divided by elapsed time.

> [!equation]
> - **Displacement:** $\displaystyle \Delta x = x_f - x_i$
> - **Average velocity:** $\displaystyle \bar{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}$

## Instantaneous Velocity and Speed
- **Instantaneous velocity** is the derivative of position — the slope of the x-vs-t graph. It is a vector and can be negative.
- **Instantaneous speed** is its absolute value; **average speed** is total distance over elapsed time.

> [!equation]
> - **Instantaneous velocity:** $\displaystyle v(t) = \frac{dx(t)}{dt}$
> - **Average speed:** $\displaystyle \bar{s} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Elapsed time}}$

## Average and Instantaneous Acceleration
- **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity (m/s²); a vector that can change speed, direction or both.
- Instantaneous acceleration is the derivative of velocity — the slope of the v-vs-t graph.
- Negative acceleration is acceleration in the negative direction of the chosen axis.

> [!equation]
> - **Average acceleration:** $\displaystyle \bar{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_0}{t_f - t_0}$
> - **Instantaneous acceleration:** $\displaystyle a(t) = \frac{dv(t)}{dt}$

## Motion with Constant Acceleration
- Identify the knowns, then pick the kinematic equation(s) that connect them; one or two may be needed.
- Two-body pursuit problems need two equations solved together.

> [!equation]
> - **Average velocity (constant a):** $\displaystyle \bar{v} = \frac{v_0 + v}{2}$
> - **Velocity from acceleration:** $\displaystyle v = v_0 + at$
> - **Position from velocity and acceleration:** $\displaystyle x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
> - **Velocity from distance:** $\displaystyle v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)$

## Free Fall
- With negligible air resistance, a falling object has constant acceleration $g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$.
- Upward is normally taken as positive.

> [!equation]
> - **Velocity of free fall:** $\displaystyle v = v_0 - gt$
> - **Height of free fall:** $\displaystyle y = y_0 + v_0 t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
> - **Velocity of free fall from height:** $\displaystyle v^2 = v_0^2 - 2g(y - y_0)$

> [!note] Connection
> Why $g$ has this value near Earth's surface is explained in [[Gravitation]].

## Finding Velocity and Displacement from Acceleration
- Integration gives velocity from acceleration and position from velocity.
- For constant acceleration these reduce to the kinematic equations above.

> [!equation]
> - **Velocity by integration:** $\displaystyle v(t) = \int a(t)\,dt + C_1$
> - **Position by integration:** $\displaystyle x(t) = \int v(t)\,dt + C_2$
