# Photons and Matter Waves

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 2 — Modern Physics › Chapter 6
> ← [[Relativity]] · Next: [[Quantum Mechanics]] →

**Photons and Matter Waves** covers the experiments that broke classical physics: light comes in quanta (photons), and particles behave like waves.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Wien's displacement law:** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\max} T = 2.898 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}\cdot\text{K}$
> - **Stefan's law:** $\displaystyle P(T) = \sigma A T^{4}$
> - **Energy quantum:** $\displaystyle \Delta E = hf$
> - **Planck's constant:** $\displaystyle h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J}\cdot\text{s}$
> - **Photon energy:** $\displaystyle E_f = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
> - **Photoelectric equation:** $\displaystyle K_{\max} = hf - \phi$
> - **Cut-off frequency:** $\displaystyle f_c = \frac{\phi}{h}$
> - **Photon momentum:** $\displaystyle p_f = \frac{h}{\lambda}$
> - **Compton shift:** $\displaystyle \Delta\lambda = \lambda_c(1 - \cos\theta)$
> - **Compton wavelength of the electron:** $\displaystyle \lambda_c = \frac{h}{m_0 c} = 0.00243\ \text{nm}$
> - **Rydberg formula:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_f^{2}} - \frac{1}{n_i^{2}}\right)$
> - **Angular momentum quantization:** $\displaystyle L_n = n\hbar$
> - **Energy of the nth orbit:** $\displaystyle E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}$
> - **Radius of the nth orbit:** $\displaystyle r_n = a_0 n^{2},\quad a_0 = 0.529\ \text{Å}$
> - **de Broglie wavelength:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{h}{p}$
> - **Heisenberg uncertainty principle:** $\displaystyle \Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \frac{1}{2}\hbar$

## Blackbody Radiation
- All bodies radiate, more strongly and at shorter peak wavelength when hotter (**Wien's law**).
- Total radiated power goes as $T^4$ (**Stefan's law**).
- Classical theory fails; **Planck** proposed energy is exchanged in discrete quanta, giving a law that matches experiment.

> [!equation]
> - **Wien's displacement law:** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\max} T = 2.898 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}\cdot\text{K}$
> - **Stefan's law:** $\displaystyle P(T) = \sigma A T^{4}$
> - **Energy quantum:** $\displaystyle \Delta E = hf$
> - **Planck's constant:** $\displaystyle h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J}\cdot\text{s}$

> [!note] Connection
> Stefan's law is the radiation part of heat transfer in [[Temperature and Heat]].

## Photoelectric Effect
- Light ejects electrons instantly, only above a cutoff frequency, with KE independent of intensity — classical theory can't explain this.
- Light is made of **photons** of energy $hf$; each gives all its energy to one electron.
- Max KE = photon energy − **work function** (the metal's binding energy).

> [!equation]
> - **Photon energy:** $\displaystyle E_f = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
> - **Photoelectric equation:** $\displaystyle K_{\max} = hf - \phi$
> - **Cut-off frequency:** $\displaystyle f_c = \frac{\phi}{h}$

## The Compton Effect
- X-rays scattered by electrons come out with longer wavelength.
- Explained by treating photon and electron as relativistic particles conserving energy and momentum — strong support for photons.

> [!equation]
> - **Photon momentum:** $\displaystyle p_f = \frac{h}{\lambda}$
> - **Compton shift:** $\displaystyle \Delta\lambda = \lambda_c(1 - \cos\theta)$
> - **Compton wavelength of the electron:** $\displaystyle \lambda_c = \frac{h}{m_0 c} = 0.00243\ \text{nm}$

> [!note] Connection
> Uses relativistic energy–momentum from [[Relativity]].

## Bohr's Model of the Hydrogen Atom
- The **Rydberg formula** fits hydrogen's spectral lines, which classical physics can't explain.
- Rutherford: nearly all mass in a small positive nucleus.
- Bohr's postulates: circular orbits, **quantized angular momentum**, and photons emitted or absorbed when jumping between orbits.
- Semi-classical, but it introduced quantum numbers and led to quantum mechanics.

> [!equation]
> - **Rydberg formula:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_f^{2}} - \frac{1}{n_i^{2}}\right)$
> - **Angular momentum quantization:** $\displaystyle L_n = n\hbar$
> - **Energy of the nth orbit:** $\displaystyle E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}$
> - **Radius of the nth orbit:** $\displaystyle r_n = a_0 n^{2},\quad a_0 = 0.529\ \text{Å}$

> [!note] Connection
> Angular momentum is from [[Angular Momentum]]. The full quantum treatment of hydrogen is in [[Atomic Structure]].

## De Broglie's Matter Waves
- Every particle with momentum has a matter wave with wavelength inversely proportional to its momentum.
- This explains Bohr's quantized angular momentum.
- Davisson–Germer: electrons diffract off a nickel crystal — evidence of matter waves.

> [!equation]
> - **de Broglie wavelength:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{h}{p}$

> [!note] Connection
> Diffraction is covered in [[Diffraction]].

## Wave-Particle Duality
- Particles and radiation act as waves or particles depending on the experiment; electron double-slit experiments confirm the wave nature.
- Particles are described as wave packets; the **uncertainty principle** limits simultaneous position and momentum knowledge.
- Applications: CCD cameras, SEM and TEM electron microscopes.

> [!equation]
> - **Heisenberg uncertainty principle:** $\displaystyle \Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \frac{1}{2}\hbar$

> [!note] Connection
> Developed further in [[Quantum Mechanics]].
