# Interference

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 1 — Optics › Chapter 3
> ← [[Geometric Optics and Image Formation]] · Next: [[Diffraction]] →

**Interference** is the result of light waves overlapping. Young's double-slit experiment proved light is a wave, and the same idea explains thin films and interferometers.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Path difference:** $\displaystyle \Delta l = d\sin\theta$
> - **Constructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = m\lambda,\quad m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots$
> - **Destructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = \left(m + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$
> - **Bright fringe position:** $\displaystyle y_m = \frac{m\lambda D}{d}$
> - **Michelson displacement:** $\displaystyle \Delta d = m\frac{\lambda_0}{2}$

## Young's Double-Slit Interference
- Young's experiment gave definitive evidence that light is a wave.
- Light from two slits superposes to make an interference pattern.

> [!note] Connection
> Superposition of waves is introduced in [[Waves]] (Volume 1).

## Mathematics of Interference
- Bright fringes where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths; dark fringes at half-integers.
- $d$ is slit separation, $\theta$ the angle, $m$ the order.

> [!equation]
> - **Path difference:** $\displaystyle \Delta l = d\sin\theta$
> - **Constructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = m\lambda,\quad m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots$
> - **Destructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = \left(m + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$
> - **Bright fringe position:** $\displaystyle y_m = \frac{m\lambda D}{d}$

## Multiple-Slit Interference
- With more than two slits there are principal and secondary maxima; more slits make principal maxima brighter and narrower.

> [!note] Connection
> Taken to many slits, this becomes the diffraction grating — [[Diffraction]].

## Interference in Thin Films
- Reflecting off a medium of higher index gives a 180° ($\lambda/2$) phase change.
- Thin-film colors come from light reflected off the top and bottom surfaces; both path difference and phase change matter.

## The Michelson Interferometer
- Moving one mirror by $\lambda/2$ shifts each fringe to the next fringe's position.

> [!equation]
> - **Michelson displacement:** $\displaystyle \Delta d = m\frac{\lambda_0}{2}$
