# Sources of Magnetic Fields

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 12
> ← [[Magnetic Forces and Fields]] · Next: [[Electromagnetic Induction]] →

**Sources of Magnetic Fields** covers how currents create magnetic fields, using the Biot-Savart law and Ampère's law, and how materials respond to magnetism.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,d\vec{l}\times\hat{r}}{r^2}$$
> $$B_{\text{wire}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$
> $$B_{\text{loop center}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$$
> $$\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$$
> $$B_{\text{solenoid}} = \mu_0 n I$$

---

## The Biot-Savart Law
The field from a small current element:

> [!equation]
> $$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,d\vec{l}\times\hat{r}}{r^2}$$

## Field of a Straight Wire
The field circles the wire, with magnitude

> [!equation]
> $$B_{\text{wire}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$

## Force Between Parallel Currents
Currents in the same direction **attract**; opposite currents **repel**. This force defines the **ampere**.

> [!note] Connection
> Each wire feels $\vec{F} = I\vec{L}\times\vec{B}$ from the other's field — see [[Magnetic Forces and Fields]]. The ampere is the unit of current in [[Current and Resistance]].

## Field of a Current Loop
At the center of a loop of radius $R$:

> [!equation]
> $$B_{\text{loop center}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$$

## Ampère's Law
> [!equation]
> $$\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$$

Like [[Gauss's Law]] for electric fields, Ampère's law gives easy results in symmetric cases.

> [!note] Connection
> Maxwell added a **displacement current** term to this law, giving the Ampère-Maxwell law in [[Electromagnetic Waves]].

## Solenoids and Toroids
Inside a solenoid, the field is uniform and nearly zero outside:

> [!equation]
> $$B_{\text{solenoid}} = \mu_0 n I$$

A toroid's field is confined to its core.

> [!note] Connection
> The self-inductance of a solenoid, $L = \mu_0N^2A/l$, is in [[Inductance]].

## Magnetism in Matter
Materials are **paramagnetic**, **diamagnetic** or **ferromagnetic**. Ferromagnets have **domains** and show **hysteresis**.
