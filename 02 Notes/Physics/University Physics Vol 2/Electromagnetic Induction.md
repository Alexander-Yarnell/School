# Electromagnetic Induction

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 13
> ← [[Sources of Magnetic Fields]] · Next: [[Inductance]] →

**Electromagnetic Induction** is the creation of an emf by a changing magnetic flux. It is how generators work, and it shows that changing magnetic fields create electric fields.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon = -N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$
> $$\varepsilon = Blv$$
> $$\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$
> $$\varepsilon = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$$

---

## Faraday's Law
A changing magnetic flux induces an emf:

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon = -N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Magnetic flux is the magnetic counterpart of the electric flux in [[Gauss's Law]]. Faraday's law is one of Maxwell's equations in [[Electromagnetic Waves]].

## Lenz's Law
The induced current **opposes the change** that caused it. This is the meaning of the minus sign in Faraday's law.

## Motional EMF
A rod of length $l$ moving at speed $v$ through a field $B$:

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon = Blv$$

## Induced Electric Fields
A changing $\vec{B}$ creates a **non-conservative** electric field, even with no wire present:

> [!equation]
> $$\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

> [!note] Connection
> This contrasts with the electrostatic force, which is conservative — see [[Electric Potential]].

## Eddy Currents
Induced swirling currents in conductors cause **magnetic braking** and heating. They are used in induction cooktops and metal detectors.

## Generators and Back EMF
A coil rotating in a magnetic field produces a sinusoidal emf:

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$$

Motors produce a **back emf** that limits their current.

> [!note] Connection
> Sinusoidal sources are the starting point of [[Alternating-Current Circuits]]. Motors run on the torque on a current loop from [[Magnetic Forces and Fields]].

## Applications
Guitar pickups, credit-card readers, wireless charging.
