# Applications of Newton's Laws

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 6
> ← [[Newton's Laws of Motion]] · Next: [[Work and Kinetic Energy]] →

**Applications of Newton's Laws** uses [[Newton's Laws of Motion]] on real situations: friction, circular motion and motion through fluids.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Static friction:** $\displaystyle f_s \le \mu_s N$
> - **Kinetic friction:** $\displaystyle f_k = \mu_k N$
> - **Centripetal force:** $\displaystyle F_c = m\frac{v^2}{r} = mr\omega^2$
> - **Ideal banking angle:** $\displaystyle \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$
> - **Drag force:** $\displaystyle F_D = \frac{1}{2}C\rho A v^2$
> - **Stokes' law:** $\displaystyle F_s = 6\pi r\eta v$

---

## Solving Problems with Newton's Laws
- Draw a free-body diagram, resolve forces into components, and decide whether the net force is $ma$ or zero.
- The normal force isn't always equal to the weight — e.g. during vertical acceleration or on an incline.
- Many problems combine kinematics and dynamics.

## Friction
- **Friction** is a contact force opposing motion or attempted motion; simple friction is proportional to the normal force.
- The coefficient of **kinetic** friction is always less than that of **static** friction; both depend on the two materials.

> [!equation]
> - **Static friction:** $\displaystyle f_s \le \mu_s N$
> - **Kinetic friction:** $\displaystyle f_k = \mu_k N$

> [!note] Connection
> Work done by kinetic friction is in [[Work and Kinetic Energy]]; friction is a non-conservative force ([[Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy]]).

## Centripetal Force
- **Centripetal force** points toward the center of rotation, perpendicular to the velocity, with $F_c = ma_c$.
- Rotating and accelerating frames are noninertial; inertial forces like the **Coriolis force** explain motion in them.

> [!equation]
> - **Centripetal force:** $\displaystyle F_c = m\frac{v^2}{r} = mr\omega^2$
> - **Ideal banking angle:** $\displaystyle \tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$

> [!note] Connection
> Centripetal acceleration $a_C = v^2/r$ comes from [[Motion in Two and Three Dimensions]]. Gravity supplies the centripetal force for orbits in [[Gravitation]].

## Drag Force and Terminal Speed
- **Drag** opposes motion through a fluid.
- For larger objects it depends on the drag coefficient, area and fluid density; for very small objects (e.g. bacteria in water) **Stokes' law** applies.

> [!equation]
> - **Drag force:** $\displaystyle F_D = \frac{1}{2}C\rho A v^2$
> - **Stokes' law:** $\displaystyle F_s = 6\pi r\eta v$

> [!note] Connection
> Viscosity $\eta$ and fluid density $\rho$ are covered in [[Fluid Mechanics]].
