# Work and Kinetic Energy

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 7
> ← [[Applications of Newton's Laws]] · Next: [[Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy]] →

**Work and Kinetic Energy** introduces energy methods: work done by forces changes an object's kinetic energy, often avoiding solving Newton's second law directly.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Work (infinitesimal):** $\displaystyle dW = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = |\vec{F}|\,|d\vec{r}|\cos\theta$
> - **Work along a path:** $\displaystyle W_{AB} = \int_{\text{path } AB} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$
> - **Work by kinetic friction:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{fr}} = -f_k\,|l_{AB}|$
> - **Work by gravity near Earth:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{grav},AB} = -mg\,(y_B - y_A)$
> - **Work by a spring:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{spring},AB} = -\tfrac{1}{2}k\left(x_B^2 - x_A^2\right)$
> - **Kinetic energy:** $\displaystyle K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$
> - **Work-energy theorem:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{net}} = K_B - K_A$
> - **Power:** $\displaystyle P = \frac{dW}{dt}$
> - **Power from force and velocity:** $\displaystyle P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$

## Work
- Work over a tiny displacement is the **dot product** of force and displacement; over a path it is the integral.
- Work done *against* a force is the negative of the work done *by* it.
- Gravity's work near Earth depends only on weight and change in height; a spring's work depends only on $k$ and the start and end positions.

> [!equation]
> - **Work (infinitesimal):** $\displaystyle dW = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = |\vec{F}|\,|d\vec{r}|\cos\theta$
> - **Work along a path:** $\displaystyle W_{AB} = \int_{\text{path } AB} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$
> - **Work by kinetic friction:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{fr}} = -f_k\,|l_{AB}|$
> - **Work by gravity near Earth:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{grav},AB} = -mg\,(y_B - y_A)$
> - **Work by a spring:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{spring},AB} = -\tfrac{1}{2}k\left(x_B^2 - x_A^2\right)$

> [!note] Connection
> The dot product comes from [[Vectors]]. In Volume 2 the same integral gives gas work $W = \int p\,dV$ ([[First Law of Thermodynamics]]).

## Kinetic Energy
- Kinetic energy (non-relativistic) is half the mass times speed squared; a system's KE is the sum over its particles.
- KE depends on the reference frame and is always positive.

> [!equation]
> - **Kinetic energy:** $\displaystyle K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$

> [!note] Connection
> $p = mv$ is momentum — [[Linear Momentum and Collisions]]. The rotational form $K = \tfrac12 I\omega^2$ is in [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]].

## Work-Energy Theorem
- The **net work** on a particle equals its change in kinetic energy.

> [!equation]
> - **Work-energy theorem:** $\displaystyle W_{\text{net}} = K_B - K_A$

## Power
- **Power** is the rate of doing work; work over an interval is the integral of power.
- Power delivered by a force is the dot product of force and velocity.

> [!equation]
> - **Power:** $\displaystyle P = \frac{dW}{dt}$
> - **Power from force and velocity:** $\displaystyle P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$

> [!note] Connection
> Electrical power $P = IV$ is in [[Current and Resistance]]; rotational power $P = \tau\omega$ is in [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]].
