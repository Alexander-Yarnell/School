# The Kinetic Theory of Gases

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 1 — Thermodynamics › Chapter 2
> ← [[Temperature and Heat]] · Next: [[First Law of Thermodynamics]] →

**The Kinetic Theory of Gases** explains the large-scale behavior of gases — pressure, volume, temperature — in terms of the motion of their molecules.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$pV = nRT = Nk_BT$$
> $$\bar{K} = \tfrac{3}{2}k_B T$$
> $$v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3k_B T}{m}}$$
> $$C_v = \tfrac{3}{2}R \ (\text{monatomic}) \qquad C_v = \tfrac{5}{2}R \ (\text{diatomic, room temp.})$$

---

## The Ideal Gas Law
The ideal gas law links macroscopic pressure, volume and temperature:

> [!equation]
> $$pV = nRT = Nk_BT$$

Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature; the **van der Waals equation** accounts for this.

## Pressure and Temperature from Molecules
**Pressure** is the average force of molecular collisions on the container walls. The average translational kinetic energy per molecule is

> [!equation]
> $$\bar{K} = \tfrac{3}{2}k_B T$$

so temperature is a measure of molecular motion.

> [!note] Connection
> This gives a molecular meaning to the temperature introduced in [[Temperature and Heat]].

## RMS Speed and Mean Free Path
> [!equation]
> $$v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3k_B T}{m}}$$

At the same temperature, lighter molecules move faster. The **mean free path** is the average distance a molecule travels between collisions.

## Equipartition and Heat Capacity
The **equipartition theorem**: each degree of freedom carries $\tfrac{1}{2}k_BT$ of energy per molecule. This predicts the molar heat capacity at constant volume:

> [!equation]
> $$C_v = \tfrac{3}{2}R \ (\text{monatomic}) \qquad C_v = \tfrac{5}{2}R \ (\text{diatomic, room temp.})$$

> [!note] Connection
> $C_v$ is used again in the [[First Law of Thermodynamics]], where $C_p = C_v + R$ and the ratio $\gamma = C_p/C_v$ governs adiabatic processes.

## The Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution
Molecules in a gas do not all move at the same speed. The **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution** describes the spread of speeds; the most probable, mean and rms speeds differ slightly.

> [!note] Connection
> Boltzmann's constant $k_B$ reappears in the microscopic definition of entropy, $S = k_B \ln W$, in the [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]].
