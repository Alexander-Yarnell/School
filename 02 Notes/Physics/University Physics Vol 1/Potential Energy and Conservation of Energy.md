# Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 8
> ← [[Work and Kinetic Energy]] · Next: [[Linear Momentum and Collisions]] →

**Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy** adds stored energy to [[Work and Kinetic Energy]], leading to conservation of mechanical energy.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Potential energy difference:** $\displaystyle \Delta U_{AB} = U_B - U_A = -W_{AB}$
> - **Gravitational PE near Earth:** $\displaystyle U(y) = mgy + \text{const.}$
> - **Spring PE:** $\displaystyle U(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2 + \text{const.}$
> - **Closed-path work (conservative):** $\displaystyle \oint \vec{F}_{\text{cons}} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0$
> - **Force from potential energy:** $\displaystyle F_l = -\frac{dU}{dl}$
> - **Conservation of energy:** $\displaystyle 0 = W_{nc,AB} = \Delta(K+U)_{AB} = \Delta E_{AB}$

---

## Potential Energy of a System
- The change in potential energy is the negative of the work done by the force.
- Only **differences** in potential energy matter, so the zero point can be chosen freely.
- Near Earth, gravitational PE is linear in height; a spring's PE is quadratic in stretch.

> [!equation]
> - **Potential energy difference:** $\displaystyle \Delta U_{AB} = U_B - U_A = -W_{AB}$
> - **Gravitational PE near Earth:** $\displaystyle U(y) = mgy + \text{const.}$
> - **Spring PE:** $\displaystyle U(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2 + \text{const.}$

> [!note] Connection
> Far from Earth, gravitational PE becomes $U = -GM_Em/r$ — see [[Gravitation]]. The electric version is in [[Electric Potential]].

## Conservative and Non-Conservative Forces
- A **conservative** force does the same work on any path, so its work around a closed loop is zero.
- A **non-conservative** force's work depends on the path (e.g. friction).
- A conservative force component equals the negative derivative of PE along that direction.

> [!equation]
> - **Closed-path work (conservative):** $\displaystyle \oint \vec{F}_{\text{cons}} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0$
> - **Force from potential energy:** $\displaystyle F_l = -\frac{dU}{dl}$

> [!note] Connection
> The same relationship links electric field and potential: $\vec{E} = -\nabla V$ in [[Electric Potential]].

## Conservation of Energy
- The change in mechanical energy equals the work done by non-conservative forces.
- If they do no work, mechanical energy $E = K + U$ stays constant.

> [!equation]
> - **Conservation of energy:** $\displaystyle 0 = W_{nc,AB} = \Delta(K+U)_{AB} = \Delta E_{AB}$

> [!note] Connection
> Including heat as energy transfer gives the [[First Law of Thermodynamics]].

## Potential Energy Diagrams and Stability
- At a **turning point**, PE equals the total mechanical energy and KE is zero.
- The force is the negative slope of the U curve. Equilibrium is **stable** at a minimum and **unstable** at a maximum.

## Sources of Energy
- Energy transfers between systems and converts between forms (kinetic, potential, thermal, electromagnetic).
- **Renewable:** wind, water, geothermal, solar. **Non-renewable:** fossil fuels, nuclear.
