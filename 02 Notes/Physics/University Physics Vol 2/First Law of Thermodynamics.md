# The First Law of Thermodynamics

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 1 — Thermodynamics › Chapter 3
> ← [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]] · Next: [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]] →

**The First Law of Thermodynamics** is energy conservation for thermal systems: energy added as heat goes into internal energy or into work done by the system.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$\Delta U = Q - W$$
> $$W = \int p\,dV$$
> $$C_p = C_v + R$$
> $$pV^{\gamma} = \text{const (adiabatic)}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$$

---

## Systems and State Variables
A system's state is described by **state variables**: pressure $p$, volume $V$, temperature $T$ and internal energy $U$. Work and heat, by contrast, depend on the **path** taken between states, not just the endpoints.

## Work
Work done by a gas is the area under the curve on a $pV$ diagram (compare work in mechanics, [[Work and Kinetic Energy]]):

> [!equation]
> $$W = \int p\,dV$$

## The First Law
> [!equation]
> $$\Delta U = Q - W$$

Heat $Q$ added to the system goes into raising its internal energy $U$ or into work $W$ done by the system.

> [!note] Connection
> Heat $Q$ is the quantity defined and measured in [[Temperature and Heat]] (e.g. $Q = mc\,\Delta T$).

## Thermodynamic Processes
| Process | What is held fixed | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isothermal | Temperature $T$ | $\Delta U = 0$ for an ideal gas |
| Isobaric | Pressure $p$ | — |
| Isochoric | Volume $V$ | $W = 0$ |
| Adiabatic | No heat flow | $Q = 0$ |
| Cyclic | Returns to start | $\Delta U = 0$ |

> [!note] Connection
> Cyclic processes are the basis of heat engines in the [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]]. The Carnot cycle there is built from two isothermal and two adiabatic processes.

## Heat Capacities of an Ideal Gas
> [!equation]
> $$C_p = C_v + R$$

The ratio $\gamma = C_p/C_v$ governs adiabatic behavior:

> [!equation]
> $$pV^{\gamma} = \text{const (adiabatic)}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Values of $C_v$ for monatomic and diatomic gases come from equipartition in the [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]].
