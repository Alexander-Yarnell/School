# Units and Measurement

**Units and Measurement** sets up the tools the rest of the book relies on: units, conversions, dimensions, estimates, and how precise a measurement is.

> - **Percent uncertainty:** $\displaystyle \text{Percent uncertainty} = \frac{\delta A}{A} \times 100\%$

## The Scope and Scale of Physics
- Physics looks for simple laws that explain natural phenomena.
- It spans a huge range of length, mass and time scales; **order of magnitude** helps track and compare them.
- Scientists describe the world through **models**, **theories** and **laws**.

## Units and Standards
- Unit systems are built from a few **base units**; all other units are **derived** from them.
- The SI base units for length, mass and time are the **meter**, **kilogram** and **second**.
- SI is metric, so conversions go in powers of 10 using metric prefixes. English units are still common in everyday U.S. use.

## Unit Conversion
- Convert by multiplying by **conversion factors** that cancel the old unit and introduce the new one.
- Squared or cubed units (areas, volumes) need the conversion factor applied the matching number of times.

## Dimensional Analysis
- A quantity's **dimension** shows which base quantities it is built from.
- Valid physical equations must be **dimensionally consistent** — a quick way to check, recall or even derive relationships.

## Estimates and Fermi Calculations
- An **estimate** is an educated guess from experience and physical reasoning.
- Strategies: build big lengths from small ones, get areas and volumes from lengths, masses from volume and density, bound the answer, keep one significant figure, and check that it makes sense.

## Significant Figures
- **Accuracy** is closeness to an accepted value; **precision** is how well repeated measurements agree (quantified by uncertainty).
- Multiplication/division: keep no more significant figures than the least precise input.
- Addition/subtraction: keep no more decimal places than the least precise input.

> [!equation]
> - **Percent uncertainty:** $\displaystyle \text{Percent uncertainty} = \frac{\delta A}{A} \times 100\%$

## Solving Problems in Physics
- **Strategy:** identify the relevant principles and plan.
- **Solution:** do the calculation, keeping units.
- **Significance:** check units, size and sign, and whether the answer makes sense.
