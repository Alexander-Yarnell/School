# University Physics Volume 3

*University Physics* is a free, three-volume, calculus-based physics series by Samuel J. Ling, William Moebs and Jeff Sanny, published by OpenStax (Rice University). **Volume 3** has two units and 11 chapters: four on **Optics** and seven on **Modern Physics**.

> [!tip] How to read these notes
> The important equations to know and use are in pink **equation** boxes. Every chapter page lists all of its key equations at the top, then repeats each one in the notes where it comes up.
> The boxes turn pink when the `pink-equations` CSS snippet is on (Settings → Appearance → CSS snippets).

## Key Equations

### Optics

> [!equation] [[The Nature of Light]]
> - **Speed of light:** $\displaystyle c \approx 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$
> - **Index of refraction:** $\displaystyle n = \frac{c}{v}$
> - **Law of reflection:** $\displaystyle \theta_r = \theta_i$
> - **Snell's law:** $\displaystyle n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$
> - **Critical angle:** $\displaystyle \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\ \text{for } n_1 > n_2$
> - **Malus's law:** $\displaystyle I = I_0 \cos^2\theta$

> [!equation] [[Geometric Optics and Image Formation]]
> - **Focal length:** $\displaystyle f = \frac{R}{2}$
> - **Mirror equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Magnification:** $\displaystyle m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$
> - **Thin-lens equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Lens maker's equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$

> [!equation] [[Interference]]
> - **Path difference:** $\displaystyle \Delta l = d\sin\theta$
> - **Constructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = m\lambda,\quad m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots$
> - **Destructive interference:** $\displaystyle d\sin\theta = \left(m + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$
> - **Bright fringe position:** $\displaystyle y_m = \frac{m\lambda D}{d}$

> [!equation] [[Diffraction]]
> - **Single-slit minima:** $\displaystyle a\sin\theta = m\lambda,\quad m = \pm1, \pm2, \ldots$
> - **Intensity:** $\displaystyle I = I_0\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2$
> - **Rayleigh criterion:** $\displaystyle \theta = 1.22\frac{\lambda}{D}$
> - **Bragg equation:** $\displaystyle m\lambda = 2d\sin\theta,\quad m = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

### Modern Physics

> [!equation] [[Relativity]]
> - **Lorentz factor:** $\displaystyle \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$
> - **Time dilation:** $\displaystyle \Delta t = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = \gamma\Delta\tau$
> - **Length contraction:** $\displaystyle L = L_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}$
> - **Lorentz transformation:** $\displaystyle t = \frac{t' + vx'/c^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},\quad x = \frac{x' + vt'}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$
> - **Relativistic velocity addition:** $\displaystyle u_x = \frac{u'_x + v}{1 + vu'_x/c^2}$
> - **Relativistic Doppler (wavelength):** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\text{obs}} = \lambda_s\sqrt{\frac{1 + v/c}{1 - v/c}}$
> - **Relativistic momentum:** $\displaystyle \vec{p} = \gamma m\vec{u} = \frac{m\vec{u}}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{u^2}{c^2}}}$
> - **Relativistic total energy:** $\displaystyle E = \gamma mc^2$
> - **Relativistic kinetic energy:** $\displaystyle K_{\text{rel}} = (\gamma - 1)mc^2$

> [!equation] [[Photons and Matter Waves]]
> - **Wien's displacement law:** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\max} T = 2.898 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}\cdot\text{K}$
> - **Energy quantum:** $\displaystyle \Delta E = hf$
> - **Photon energy:** $\displaystyle E_f = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
> - **Photoelectric equation:** $\displaystyle K_{\max} = hf - \phi$
> - **Photon momentum:** $\displaystyle p_f = \frac{h}{\lambda}$
> - **Compton shift:** $\displaystyle \Delta\lambda = \lambda_c(1 - \cos\theta)$
> - **Rydberg formula:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_f^{2}} - \frac{1}{n_i^{2}}\right)$
> - **Energy of the nth orbit:** $\displaystyle E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}$
> - **de Broglie wavelength:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{h}{p}$
> - **Heisenberg uncertainty principle:** $\displaystyle \Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \frac{1}{2}\hbar$

> [!equation] [[Quantum Mechanics]]
> - **Normalization:** $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Psi(x,t)|^2\,dx = 1$
> - **Position–momentum uncertainty:** $\displaystyle \Delta x\,\Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$
> - **Time-independent Schrödinger equation:** $\displaystyle -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + U(x)\psi = E\psi$
> - **Allowed energies:** $\displaystyle E_n = n^2\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2},\quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
> - **Energy levels:** $\displaystyle E_n = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega,\quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$
> - **Transmission coefficient (approx.):** $\displaystyle T(L,E) = 16\frac{E}{U_0}\left(1 - \frac{E}{U_0}\right)e^{-2\beta L}$

> [!equation] [[Atomic Structure]]
> - **Orbital angular momentum:** $\displaystyle L = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\,\hbar$
> - **z-component:** $\displaystyle L_z = m\hbar$
> - **Electrons per subshell:** $\displaystyle N = 4l + 2$

> [!equation] [[Condensed Matter Physics]]
> - **Rotational energy:** $\displaystyle E_r = l(l+1)\frac{\hbar^2}{2I}$
> - **Fermi energy:** $\displaystyle E_F = \frac{h^2}{8m_e}\left(\frac{3N}{\pi V}\right)^{2/3}$
> - **Hall voltage:** $\displaystyle V_H = uBw$
> - **Diode current:** $\displaystyle I_{\text{net}} = I_0\left(e^{eV_b/k_BT} - 1\right)$

> [!equation] [[Nuclear Physics]]
> - **Mass number:** $\displaystyle A = Z + N$
> - **Binding energy:** $\displaystyle E = (\Delta m)c^2$
> - **Radioactive decay law:** $\displaystyle N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
> - **Decay constant and half-life:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{0.693}{T_{1/2}}$
> - **Alpha decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}X + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$
> - **Beta decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}X + {}^{0}_{-1}e + \bar{\nu}$

> [!equation] [[Particle Physics and Cosmology]]
> - **Momentum in a cyclotron:** $\displaystyle p = 0.3Br$
> - **Hubble's law:** $\displaystyle v = H_0 d$

## Unit 1 — Optics

How light travels, reflects, refracts and forms images, and what its wave nature does: interference and diffraction.

1. [[The Nature of Light]] — speed of light; reflection; refraction; total internal reflection; dispersion; Huygens's principle; polarization.
2. [[Geometric Optics and Image Formation]] — plane and spherical mirrors; refraction at surfaces; thin lenses; the eye; cameras; magnifiers; microscopes and telescopes.
3. [[Interference]] — Young's double slit; interference conditions; multiple slits; thin films; the Michelson interferometer.
4. [[Diffraction]] — single-slit diffraction and intensity; double-slit diffraction; gratings; resolution; X-ray diffraction; holography.

## Unit 2 — Modern Physics

Physics beyond the classical picture: relativity, photons and matter waves, quantum mechanics, and atoms, solids, nuclei, particles and the universe.

5. [[Relativity]] — postulates; simultaneity; time dilation; length contraction; Lorentz transformation; velocity addition; Doppler for light; relativistic momentum and energy.
6. [[Photons and Matter Waves]] — blackbody radiation; photoelectric effect; Compton effect; Bohr's hydrogen atom; de Broglie matter waves; wave-particle duality.
7. [[Quantum Mechanics]] — wave functions; uncertainty principle; Schrödinger equation; particle in a box; quantum harmonic oscillator; tunneling.
8. [[Atomic Structure]] — the hydrogen atom; orbital magnetic moment; electron spin; exclusion principle and the periodic table; spectra and X-rays; lasers.
9. [[Condensed Matter Physics]] — molecular bonds; molecular spectra; crystals; free electron model; band theory; semiconductors and devices; superconductivity.
10. [[Nuclear Physics]] — properties of nuclei; binding energy; radioactive decay; nuclear reactions; fission; fusion; medical uses and biological effects.
11. [[Particle Physics and Cosmology]] — fundamental forces and particles; conservation laws; quarks; accelerators and detectors; the Standard Model; the Big Bang; the early universe.
