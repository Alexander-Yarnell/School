# Electric Charges and Fields

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 5
> ← [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]] · Next: [[Gauss's Law]] →

**Electric Charges and Fields** opens the electricity and magnetism unit: what charge is, the force between charges (Coulomb's law), and the electric field.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad k = 8.99\times10^{9}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$$
> $$\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}$$
> $$E_{\text{point}} = k\frac{q}{r^2}$$
> $$E_{\text{plane}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$$
> $$p = qd \qquad \vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$$

---

## Electric Charge
Charge comes in two kinds, positive and negative. It is **quantized** in units of $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C, and it is **conserved**.

## Conductors and Insulators
Charges move freely in **conductors** but not in **insulators**. Objects can be charged by friction, by conduction, or by **induction** (polarizing an object, then grounding it).

## Coulomb's Law
The force between two point charges:

> [!equation]
> $$F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad k = 8.99\times10^{9}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$$

Forces from many charges add as vectors (**superposition**). Coulomb's law has the same inverse-square form as [[Gravitation]].

> [!note] Connection
> The electric force is conservative, which allows the potential energy $U = kq_1q_2/r$ in [[Electric Potential]].

## The Electric Field
The electric field is the force per unit charge:

> [!equation]
> $$\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}$$

For a point charge:

> [!equation]
> $$E_{\text{point}} = k\frac{q}{r^2}$$

## Fields of Continuous Distributions
For extended charge, integrate $d\vec{E}$ over a line (charge density $\lambda$), a surface ($\sigma$) or a volume ($\rho$). Worked cases include a finite line, a ring, a disk, and an infinite plane:

> [!equation]
> $$E_{\text{plane}} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$$

> [!note] Connection
> [[Gauss's Law]] finds fields like the infinite plane and infinite line much more easily by using symmetry.

## Field Lines
Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative charges. Their density shows the field's strength.

## Electric Dipoles
A dipole has dipole moment $p = qd$ and feels a torque in an electric field:

> [!equation]
> $$p = qd \qquad \vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$$

> [!note] Connection
> A current loop in a magnetic field feels a torque of the same form, $\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}$ — see [[Magnetic Forces and Fields]].
