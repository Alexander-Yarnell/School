# Magnetic Forces and Fields

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 11
> ← [[Direct-Current Circuits]] · Next: [[Sources of Magnetic Fields]] →

**Magnetic Forces and Fields** covers what a magnetic field does: the force it exerts on moving charges and on currents, and the torque on current loops.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$$
> $$r = \frac{mv}{qB}$$
> $$\vec{F} = I\vec{L}\times\vec{B}$$
> $$\mu = NIA \qquad \vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}$$

---

## Magnetic Fields
Magnetic fields are produced by moving charges and by magnets, and are measured in **teslas**. Field lines form closed loops, leaving north poles and entering south poles.

> [!note] Connection
> How currents produce $\vec{B}$ is the subject of [[Sources of Magnetic Fields]].

## Force on a Moving Charge
> [!equation]
> $$\vec{F} = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$$

The force is perpendicular to both $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{B}$ (right-hand rule), so it **does no work**.

## Circular and Helical Motion
A charge moving perpendicular to $\vec{B}$ moves in a circle of radius

> [!equation]
> $$r = \frac{mv}{qB}$$

If it moves at an angle to $\vec{B}$, it spirals in a **helix**. This explains auroras and magnetic traps.

## Force on a Current-Carrying Wire
> [!equation]
> $$\vec{F} = I\vec{L}\times\vec{B}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Two parallel wires exert this force on each other — see [[Sources of Magnetic Fields]].

## Torque on a Current Loop
A loop has **magnetic dipole moment** $\mu = NIA$ and feels a torque:

> [!equation]
> $$\mu = NIA \qquad \vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}$$

This is the principle behind **electric motors**.

> [!note] Connection
> Compare the electric dipole torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p}\times\vec{E}$ in [[Electric Charges and Fields]]. Running motors also produce a **back emf** — see [[Electromagnetic Induction]].

## The Hall Effect
A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field develops a sideways voltage. This **Hall voltage** reveals the sign and density of the charge carriers.

> [!note] Connection
> Carrier density $n$ appears in the current density $J = nqv_d$ in [[Current and Resistance]]. The Hall effect in semiconductors is in [[Condensed Matter Physics]].

## Applications
Velocity selectors, mass spectrometers, cyclotrons.
