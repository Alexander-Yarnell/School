# Temperature and Heat

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 1 — Thermodynamics › Chapter 1
> ← [[Sound]] (end of Vol 1) · Next: [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]] →

**Temperature and Heat** is the opening chapter of the thermodynamics unit. It covers thermal equilibrium, temperature scales, thermal expansion, heat and calorimetry, phase changes, and the three ways heat moves.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$$
> $$\Delta L = \alpha L\,\Delta T \qquad \Delta V = \beta V\,\Delta T$$
> $$Q = mc\,\Delta T$$
> $$Q = mL$$
> $$\text{Conduction rate} = \frac{kA\,\Delta T}{d}$$
> $$P_{\text{rad}} = \sigma e A T^4$$

---

## Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium
Objects in contact exchange heat until they reach the same temperature — they are then in **thermal equilibrium**.

The **zeroth law of thermodynamics**: if A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with C, then A and B are in equilibrium with each other. This is what makes thermometers work.

## Temperature Scales
The three scales are **Celsius**, **Fahrenheit** and **Kelvin**. Kelvin is the absolute scale: 0 K is absolute zero.

> [!equation]
> $$T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$$

> [!note] Connection
> The [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]] explains what temperature *is* at the molecular level: it measures the average kinetic energy of molecular motion.

## Thermal Expansion
Most materials grow when heated.

> [!equation]
> - Linear change: $\displaystyle \Delta L = \alpha L\,\Delta T$
> - Volume change: $\displaystyle \Delta V = \beta V\,\Delta T$, with $\beta \approx 3\alpha$ for solids.

When expansion is blocked, the result is **thermal stress**.

## Heat and Calorimetry
**Heat** $Q$ is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. The temperature change it produces depends on the **specific heat** $c$:

> [!equation]
> $$Q = mc\,\Delta T$$

In an isolated **calorimeter**, heat lost by one part equals heat gained by the other.

> [!note] Connection
> Heat is one of the two ways energy enters or leaves a system in the [[First Law of Thermodynamics]] ($\Delta U = Q - W$).

## Phase Changes
Temperature stays constant while a substance melts or boils. The heat needed uses the **latent heat** (of fusion or of vaporization):

> [!equation]
> $$Q = mL$$

Phase diagrams show the **triple point** and **critical point**.

## Heat Transfer
| Mode | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Conduction | Through a material; rate $= kA\,\Delta T/d$ |
| Convection | Bulk motion of a fluid |
| Radiation | Emitted energy, given by the Stefan-Boltzmann law |

> [!equation]
> $$\text{Conduction rate} = \frac{kA\,\Delta T}{d}$$
> $$P_{\text{rad}} = \sigma e A T^4$$

> [!note] Connection
> Heat flowing on its own goes from hot to cold. The Clausius statement of the [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]] says it never flows spontaneously the other way, and heat flowing across a finite temperature difference is one of that chapter's examples of an irreversible process.
