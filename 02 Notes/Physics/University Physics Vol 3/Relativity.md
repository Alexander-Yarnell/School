# Relativity

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 2 — Modern Physics › Chapter 5
> ← [[Diffraction]] · Next: [[Photons and Matter Waves]] →

**Relativity** opens modern physics. Special relativity starts from two postulates — the laws of physics and the speed of light are the same in every inertial frame — and follows them to time dilation, length contraction and $E = mc^2$.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Lorentz factor:** $\displaystyle \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$
> - **Time dilation:** $\displaystyle \Delta t = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = \gamma\Delta\tau$
> - **Length contraction:** $\displaystyle L = L_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}$
> - **Galilean transformation:** $\displaystyle x = x' + vt,\quad t = t'$
> - **Lorentz transformation:** $\displaystyle t = \frac{t' + vx'/c^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},\quad x = \frac{x' + vt'}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$
> - **Spacetime interval:** $\displaystyle (\Delta s)^2 = (\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2 + (\Delta z)^2 - c^2(\Delta t)^2$
> - **Relativistic velocity addition:** $\displaystyle u_x = \frac{u'_x + v}{1 + vu'_x/c^2}$
> - **Relativistic Doppler (wavelength):** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\text{obs}} = \lambda_s\sqrt{\frac{1 + v/c}{1 - v/c}}$
> - **Relativistic Doppler (frequency):** $\displaystyle f_{\text{obs}} = f_s\sqrt{\frac{1 - v/c}{1 + v/c}}$
> - **Relativistic momentum:** $\displaystyle \vec{p} = \gamma m\vec{u} = \frac{m\vec{u}}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{u^2}{c^2}}}$
> - **Relativistic total energy:** $\displaystyle E = \gamma mc^2$
> - **Relativistic kinetic energy:** $\displaystyle K_{\text{rel}} = (\gamma - 1)mc^2$

## Invariance of Physical Laws
- Special relativity covers uniform motion; general relativity covers acceleration and gravity.
- Postulates: physical laws are the same in all inertial frames, and the speed of light is the same in all inertial frames.
- The Michelson–Morley experiment found the speed of light doesn't depend on Earth's motion.
- At low speeds and weak gravity, relativity agrees with Galilean (classical) relativity.

> [!note] Connection
> Inertial frames come from [[Newton's Laws of Motion]]; general relativity is introduced in [[Gravitation]].

## Relativity of Simultaneity
- Events simultaneous for one observer are not necessarily simultaneous for an observer in another frame.

## Time Dilation
- Moving clocks run slow: the interval is longest for an observer who sees the events at different places.
- **Proper time** $\tau$ is measured where both events happen at the same place — the shortest interval.
- The twin paradox isn't symmetric because the traveling twin accelerates.

> [!equation]
> - **Lorentz factor:** $\displaystyle \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$
> - **Time dilation:** $\displaystyle \Delta t = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = \gamma\Delta\tau$

## Length Contraction
- An object moving relative to the observer is measured shorter along its motion.
- **Proper length** (measured at rest) is the longest length.

> [!equation]
> - **Length contraction:** $\displaystyle L = L_0\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}$

## The Lorentz Transformation
- Galilean transformations assume universal time and fixed lengths, which conflicts with the postulates.
- The Lorentz transformation relates position and time between frames correctly.
- In 4D spacetime the invariant is the spacetime interval $\Delta s$, not the spatial distance.

> [!equation]
> - **Galilean transformation:** $\displaystyle x = x' + vt,\quad t = t'$
> - **Lorentz transformation:** $\displaystyle t = \frac{t' + vx'/c^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},\quad x = \frac{x' + vt'}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$
> - **Spacetime interval:** $\displaystyle (\Delta s)^2 = (\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2 + (\Delta z)^2 - c^2(\Delta t)^2$

> [!note] Connection
> Galilean (relative) velocity addition is in [[Motion in Two and Three Dimensions]].

## Relativistic Velocity Transformation
- Velocities don't simply add at high speed — they can never add to more than $c$.

> [!equation]
> - **Relativistic velocity addition:** $\displaystyle u_x = \frac{u'_x + v}{1 + vu'_x/c^2}$

## Doppler Effect for Light
- A receding source is **redshifted** (longer wavelength); an approaching source is **blueshifted**.

> [!equation]
> - **Relativistic Doppler (wavelength):** $\displaystyle \lambda_{\text{obs}} = \lambda_s\sqrt{\frac{1 + v/c}{1 - v/c}}$
> - **Relativistic Doppler (frequency):** $\displaystyle f_{\text{obs}} = f_s\sqrt{\frac{1 - v/c}{1 + v/c}}$

> [!note] Connection
> Compare the Doppler effect for sound in [[Sound]]. Redshift measures galaxy recession in [[Particle Physics and Cosmology]].

## Relativistic Momentum
- Relativistic momentum is conserved when the net external force is zero, and equals classical momentum at low speed.
- It grows without limit as $u \to c$, so nothing with mass reaches the speed of light.

> [!equation]
> - **Relativistic momentum:** $\displaystyle \vec{p} = \gamma m\vec{u} = \frac{m\vec{u}}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{u^2}{c^2}}}$

> [!note] Connection
> Classical momentum is in [[Linear Momentum and Collisions]].

## Relativistic Energy
- Total energy $E = \gamma mc^2$; rest energy $E_0 = mc^2$ — mass is a form of energy.
- Kinetic energy is $(\gamma - 1)mc^2$, which reduces to $\tfrac12 mv^2$ at low speed.
- Energy and momentum are linked by $E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2$.

> [!equation]
> - **Relativistic total energy:** $\displaystyle E = \gamma mc^2$
> - **Relativistic kinetic energy:** $\displaystyle K_{\text{rel}} = (\gamma - 1)mc^2$

> [!note] Connection
> Mass-to-energy conversion powers nuclear binding energy, fission and fusion — [[Nuclear Physics]]. Classical KE is in [[Work and Kinetic Energy]].
