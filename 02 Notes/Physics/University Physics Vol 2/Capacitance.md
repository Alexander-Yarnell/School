# Capacitance

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 8
> ← [[Electric Potential]] · Next: [[Current and Resistance]] →

**Capacitance** is a device's ability to store charge for a given voltage. Capacitors store energy in their electric field.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$
> $$C_{\text{plate}} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$
> $$C_p = C_1 + C_2 + \cdots \qquad \frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \cdots$$
> $$U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$$
> $$u_E = \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2$$
> $$C = \kappa C_0$$

---

## Capacitance
> [!equation]
> $$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$

Measured in **farads**. For a parallel-plate capacitor:

> [!equation]
> $$C_{\text{plate}} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$$

Spherical and cylindrical geometries are also derived in the chapter.

> [!note] Connection
> $V$ here is the potential difference from [[Electric Potential]].

## Series and Parallel
> [!equation]
> $$C_p = C_1 + C_2 + \cdots \qquad \frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \cdots$$

Parallel capacitors add directly; series capacitors add as reciprocals.

> [!tip] Compare
> This is the reverse of resistors in [[Direct-Current Circuits]], where *series* resistances add directly and *parallel* ones add as reciprocals.

## Stored Energy
> [!equation]
> $$U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$$

The energy is stored in the electric field, with energy density

> [!equation]
> $$u_E = \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2$$

> [!note] Connection
> Inductors store energy in the magnetic field in the parallel form $U = \tfrac{1}{2}LI^2$, $u = B^2/2\mu_0$ — see [[Inductance]].

## Dielectrics
Placing an insulator (a **dielectric**) between the plates raises the capacitance by a factor $\kappa$:

> [!equation]
> $$C = \kappa C_0$$

The dielectric's molecules polarize, creating induced charge that weakens the field inside.

## Capacitors in Circuits
- [[Direct-Current Circuits]] — RC charging and discharging, $\tau = RC$
- [[Inductance]] — LC oscillations, energy moving between capacitor and inductor
- [[Alternating-Current Circuits]] — capacitive reactance $X_C = 1/\omega C$
