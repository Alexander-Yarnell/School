# Angular Momentum

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 11
> ← [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]] · Next: [[Static Equilibrium and Elasticity]] →

**Angular Momentum** is the rotational counterpart of [[Linear Momentum and Collisions]]: torque changes it, and with no external torque it is conserved.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Rolling without slipping:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{CM}} = R\omega,\quad a_{\text{CM}} = R\alpha$
> - **Acceleration rolling down an incline:** $\displaystyle a_{\text{CM}} = \frac{mg\sin\theta}{m + (I_{\text{CM}}/r^2)}$
> - **Angular momentum (particle):** $\displaystyle \vec{l} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$
> - **Torque and angular momentum:** $\displaystyle \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = \sum \vec{\tau}$
> - **Angular momentum (rigid body):** $\displaystyle L = I\omega$
> - **Conservation of angular momentum:** $\displaystyle \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \vec{L} = \text{constant}$
> - **Precessional angular velocity:** $\displaystyle \omega_P = \frac{rMg}{I\omega}$

## Rolling Motion
- **Rolling without slipping** involves static friction; then the center-of-mass motion is tied to the rotation.
- With slipping, kinetic friction acts, those relations fail and energy is lost to heat.

> [!equation]
> - **Rolling without slipping:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{CM}} = R\omega,\quad a_{\text{CM}} = R\alpha$
> - **Acceleration rolling down an incline:** $\displaystyle a_{\text{CM}} = \frac{mg\sin\theta}{m + (I_{\text{CM}}/r^2)}$

> [!note] Connection
> Friction types are in [[Applications of Newton's Laws]].

## Angular Momentum
- A particle's angular momentum is the cross product of position and linear momentum; a system's is the vector sum.
- Net torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum.
- A rigid body has $L = I\omega$ along the rotation axis.

> [!equation]
> - **Angular momentum (particle):** $\displaystyle \vec{l} = \vec{r} \times \vec{p}$
> - **Torque and angular momentum:** $\displaystyle \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = \sum \vec{\tau}$
> - **Angular momentum (rigid body):** $\displaystyle L = I\omega$

> [!note] Connection
> Torque and $I$ come from [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]].

## Conservation of Angular Momentum
- With no external torque, total angular momentum is conserved.
- For a rigid body, $I_i\omega_i = I_f\omega_f$ — reducing $I$ increases $\omega$.
- All angular momenta must be taken about a common axis.

> [!equation]
> - **Conservation of angular momentum:** $\displaystyle \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \vec{L} = \text{constant}$

> [!note] Connection
> Kepler's second law (constant areal velocity) reflects this — see [[Gravitation]]. Bohr quantized angular momentum in the hydrogen atom — [[Photons and Matter Waves]].

## Precession of a Gyroscope
- A spinning gyroscope precesses about a vertical axis because gravity's torque is horizontal and perpendicular to $\vec L$.
- A non-spinning one simply falls over.

> [!equation]
> - **Precessional angular velocity:** $\displaystyle \omega_P = \frac{rMg}{I\omega}$
