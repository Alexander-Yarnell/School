# Condensed Matter Physics

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 2 — Modern Physics › Chapter 9
> ← [[Atomic Structure]] · Next: [[Nuclear Physics]] →

**Condensed Matter Physics** extends atomic physics to molecules and solids: how atoms bond, why metals conduct, and how semiconductors and superconductors work.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Coulomb energy at equilibrium:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{coul}} = -\frac{ke^2}{r_0}$
> - **Exclusion-principle repulsion:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{ex}} = \frac{A}{r^n}$
> - **Rotational energy:** $\displaystyle E_r = l(l+1)\frac{\hbar^2}{2I}$
> - **Moment of inertia (diatomic):** $\displaystyle I = \mu r_0^2$
> - **Dissociation energy:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{diss}} = \alpha\frac{ke^2}{r_0}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$
> - **Electron energy in a metal:** $\displaystyle E = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}\left(n_1^2 + n_2^2 + n_3^2\right)$
> - **Fermi energy:** $\displaystyle E_F = \frac{h^2}{8m_e}\left(\frac{3N}{\pi V}\right)^{2/3}$
> - **Fermi temperature:** $\displaystyle T_F = \frac{E_F}{k_B}$
> - **Hall voltage:** $\displaystyle V_H = uBw$
> - **Diode current:** $\displaystyle I_{\text{net}} = I_0\left(e^{eV_b/k_BT} - 1\right)$
> - **Transistor current gain:** $\displaystyle I_c = \beta I_B$
> - **Critical magnetic field:** $\displaystyle B_c(T) = B_c(0)\left[1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right]$

## Types of Molecular Bonds
- **Ionic** bonds transfer an electron; **covalent** bonds share electrons.
- Ionic bond energy combines ionization, electron capture and Coulomb attraction.
- Bonding combines wave functions (**hybridization**).

> [!equation]
> - **Coulomb energy at equilibrium:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{coul}} = -\frac{ke^2}{r_0}$
> - **Exclusion-principle repulsion:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{ex}} = \frac{A}{r^n}$

> [!note] Connection
> Coulomb's law is in [[Electric Charges and Fields]]; the exclusion principle is in [[Atomic Structure]].

## Molecular Spectra
- Molecules have vibrational and rotational energy; vibrational gaps are larger than rotational ones.
- Absorption-peak spacing is inversely related to moment of inertia; transitions follow selection rules.

> [!equation]
> - **Rotational energy:** $\displaystyle E_r = l(l+1)\frac{\hbar^2}{2I}$
> - **Moment of inertia (diatomic):** $\displaystyle I = \mu r_0^2$

> [!note] Connection
> Moment of inertia is from [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]]; vibrations follow the [[Quantum Mechanics]] harmonic oscillator.

## Bonding in Crystalline Solids
- Common ionic salts pack in FCC or BCC structures.
- Dissociation energy is large when the equilibrium separation is small.

> [!equation]
> - **Dissociation energy:** $\displaystyle U_{\text{diss}} = \alpha\frac{ke^2}{r_0}\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$

## Free Electron Model of Metals
- Metals conduct because many nearly free electrons move and collide randomly.
- Electron energies are quantized and high even at 0 K; the density of states grows with energy.
- Two electrons per level (spin up/down); the highest filled level at 0 K is the **Fermi energy**.

> [!equation]
> - **Electron energy in a metal:** $\displaystyle E = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}\left(n_1^2 + n_2^2 + n_3^2\right)$
> - **Fermi energy:** $\displaystyle E_F = \frac{h^2}{8m_e}\left(\frac{3N}{\pi V}\right)^{2/3}$
> - **Fermi temperature:** $\displaystyle T_F = \frac{E_F}{k_B}$

> [!note] Connection
> A 3D version of the particle in a box — [[Quantum Mechanics]]. Classical conduction in metals is in [[Current and Resistance]].

## Band Theory of Solids
- A crystal's energy levels form continuous **bands** separated by **gaps**; this structure decides whether it conducts.

## Semiconductors and Doping
- **Doping** changes the energy structure: n-type adds filled levels just below the conduction band; p-type adds levels just above the valence band.
- The **Hall effect** reveals carrier charge, drift velocity and density.

> [!equation]
> - **Hall voltage:** $\displaystyle V_H = uBw$

> [!note] Connection
> The Hall effect is introduced in [[Magnetic Forces and Fields]].

## Semiconductor Devices
- A p-n junction is a **diode**: current flows one way, rising exponentially under forward bias.
- An n-p-n junction is a **transistor**, an electrical valve used in amplifiers and computers.

> [!equation]
> - **Diode current:** $\displaystyle I_{\text{net}} = I_0\left(e^{eV_b/k_BT} - 1\right)$
> - **Transistor current gain:** $\displaystyle I_c = \beta I_B$

## Superconductivity
- Superconductors have zero resistance and repel magnetic field lines, below a critical temperature.
- A strong magnetic field destroys superconductivity; **Cooper pairs** explain it.

> [!equation]
> - **Critical magnetic field:** $\displaystyle B_c(T) = B_c(0)\left[1 - \left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^2\right]$

> [!note] Connection
> Superconductors are introduced in [[Current and Resistance]].
