# Sound

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 2 — Waves and Acoustics › Chapter 17
> ← [[Waves]] · Next: [[Temperature and Heat]] →

**Sound** is a longitudinal pressure wave. This chapter applies [[Waves]] to sound: its speed, loudness, resonance in pipes, beats, the Doppler effect and shock waves.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Pressure in a sound wave:** $\displaystyle \Delta P = \Delta P_{\max}\sin(kx \mp \omega t + \phi)$
> - **Displacement in a sound wave:** $\displaystyle s(x,t) = s_{\max}\cos(kx \mp \omega t + \phi)$
> - **Wave speed:** $\displaystyle v = f\lambda$
> - **Speed in a fluid:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}}$
> - **Speed in a solid:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$
> - **Speed in an ideal gas:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT}{M}}$
> - **Speed in air vs temperature:** $\displaystyle v = 331\ \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\sqrt{\frac{T_K}{273\ \text{K}}}$
> - **Intensity of sound:** $\displaystyle I = \frac{(\Delta p_{\max})^2}{2\rho v}$
> - **Sound intensity level:** $\displaystyle \beta\,(\text{dB}) = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$
> - **Intensity vs distance:** $\displaystyle I_2 = I_1\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$
> - **Tube closed at one end:** $\displaystyle f_n = n\frac{v}{4L},\quad n = 1, 3, 5, \ldots$
> - **Tube open at both ends:** $\displaystyle f_n = n\frac{v}{2L},\quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
> - **Beat frequency:** $\displaystyle f_{\text{beat}} = |f_2 - f_1|$
> - **Doppler shift:** $\displaystyle f_o = f_s\left(\frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}\right)$
> - **Mach number:** $\displaystyle M = \frac{v_s}{v}$
> - **Shock-wave angle:** $\displaystyle \sin\theta = \frac{v}{v_s} = \frac{1}{M}$

## Sound Waves
- Sound is a pressure wave; it can be described by pressure or by the displacement of molecules.
- Human hearing covers about 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

> [!equation]
> - **Pressure in a sound wave:** $\displaystyle \Delta P = \Delta P_{\max}\sin(kx \mp \omega t + \phi)$
> - **Displacement in a sound wave:** $\displaystyle s(x,t) = s_{\max}\cos(kx \mp \omega t + \phi)$

## Speed of Sound
- Speed depends on the medium. Fluids carry only longitudinal sound; solids carry both kinds.
- In air, speed rises with temperature (about 331 m/s at 0 °C) and is the same for all frequencies.

> [!equation]
> - **Wave speed:** $\displaystyle v = f\lambda$
> - **Speed in a fluid:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}}$
> - **Speed in a solid:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$
> - **Speed in an ideal gas:** $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT}{M}}$
> - **Speed in air vs temperature:** $\displaystyle v = 331\ \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\sqrt{\frac{T_K}{273\ \text{K}}}$

> [!note] Connection
> $\gamma$, $R$ and $T$ are the ideal-gas quantities from [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]] and [[First Law of Thermodynamics]]; $Y$ and $B$ are from [[Static Equilibrium and Elasticity]].

## Sound Intensity
- Intensity is power per area (W/m²) and depends on pressure amplitude, density and speed.
- Intensity **level** in decibels compares to the threshold of hearing, $I_0 = 10^{-12}$ W/m².
- Perceived frequency is **pitch**; perceived intensity is **loudness**.

> [!equation]
> - **Intensity of sound:** $\displaystyle I = \frac{(\Delta p_{\max})^2}{2\rho v}$
> - **Sound intensity level:** $\displaystyle \beta\,(\text{dB}) = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$
> - **Intensity vs distance:** $\displaystyle I_2 = I_1\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$

## Normal Modes of a Standing Sound Wave
- Sound shows the same interference and resonance as other waves; destructive interference can cancel noise.
- In air columns the lowest resonance is the **fundamental**; higher ones are **overtones** (together, **harmonics**).

> [!equation]
> - **Tube closed at one end:** $\displaystyle f_n = n\frac{v}{4L},\quad n = 1, 3, 5, \ldots$
> - **Tube open at both ends:** $\displaystyle f_n = n\frac{v}{2L},\quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

> [!note] Connection
> Compare standing waves on strings in [[Waves]].

## Sources of Musical Sound
- Instruments act as pipes with symmetric (open–open or closed–closed) or antisymmetric (closed–open) boundaries.
- Strings have nodes at both ends; their frequencies follow the symmetric pipe pattern, with string speed set by tension and mass per length.

## Beats
- Two sounds of slightly different frequency produce **beats** at the difference frequency.

> [!equation]
> - **Beat frequency:** $\displaystyle f_{\text{beat}} = |f_2 - f_1|$

## The Doppler Effect
- The observed frequency changes when the source or observer moves; the change is the **Doppler shift**.

> [!equation]
> - **Doppler shift:** $\displaystyle f_o = f_s\left(\frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}\right)$

> [!note] Connection
> Light has its own relativistic Doppler effect — [[Relativity]].

## Shock Waves
- A source faster than sound makes a **shock wave**; on the ground it is heard as a **sonic boom**.
- A boat's **bow wake** is the water-wave version.

> [!equation]
> - **Mach number:** $\displaystyle M = \frac{v_s}{v}$
> - **Shock-wave angle:** $\displaystyle \sin\theta = \frac{v}{v_s} = \frac{1}{M}$
