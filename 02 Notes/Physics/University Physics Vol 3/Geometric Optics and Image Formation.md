# Geometric Optics and Image Formation

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 1 — Optics › Chapter 2
> ← [[The Nature of Light]] · Next: [[Interference]] →

**Geometric Optics and Image Formation** uses rays from [[The Nature of Light]] to explain how mirrors and lenses form images, and how the eye, cameras, microscopes and telescopes work.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Plane mirror:** $\displaystyle d_o = -d_i$
> - **Focal length:** $\displaystyle f = \frac{R}{2}$
> - **Mirror equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Magnification:** $\displaystyle m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$
> - **Apparent depth:** $\displaystyle h_i = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right) h_o$
> - **Spherical interface:** $\displaystyle \frac{n_1}{d_o} + \frac{n_2}{d_i} = \frac{n_2 - n_1}{R}$
> - **Thin-lens equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Lens maker's equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> - **Optical power:** $\displaystyle P = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Angular magnification:** $\displaystyle M = \frac{\theta_{\text{image}}}{\theta_{\text{object}}}$
> - **Net magnification of a microscope:** $\displaystyle M_{\text{net}} = m^{\text{obj}} M^{\text{eye}} = -\frac{d_i^{\text{obj}}\left(f^{\text{eye}} + 25\ \text{cm}\right)}{f^{\text{obj}} f^{\text{eye}}}$

## Images Formed by Plane Mirrors
- A plane mirror forms a **virtual**, upright, same-size image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

> [!equation]
> - **Plane mirror:** $\displaystyle d_o = -d_i$

## Spherical Mirrors
- Mirrors are **concave** (converging) or **convex** (diverging); the focal length is half the radius of curvature.
- The mirror equation and ray tracing describe the image.
- Spherical mirrors show spherical aberration (parabolic ones don't); both show comatic aberration.
- Sign convention: $f$ + for concave; $d_o$, $d_i$ + for real; $m$ + for upright.

> [!equation]
> - **Focal length:** $\displaystyle f = \frac{R}{2}$
> - **Mirror equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Magnification:** $\displaystyle m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$

## Images Formed by Refraction
- Objects seen through a flat interface appear at a different depth.
- A spherical interface between media forms an image whose distance depends on the curvature, the object position and both indices.

> [!equation]
> - **Apparent depth:** $\displaystyle h_i = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right) h_o$
> - **Spherical interface:** $\displaystyle \frac{n_1}{d_o} + \frac{n_2}{d_i} = \frac{n_2 - n_1}{R}$

> [!note] Connection
> Built on Snell's law — [[The Nature of Light]].

## Thin Lenses
- **Converging** lenses bend light toward the axis; **diverging** lenses bend it away.
- Real images can be projected; virtual images can't. Converging lenses make either; diverging lenses make only virtual images.

> [!equation]
> - **Thin-lens equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> - **Lens maker's equation:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> - **Optical power:** $\displaystyle P = \frac{1}{f}$

## The Eye
- The eye forms a real image on the retina, adjusting focal length by **accommodation**.
- **Myopia** (nearsighted) is corrected with a diverging lens; **hyperopia** (farsighted) with a converging lens.

## The Camera
- Cameras use lens combinations; digital cameras use CCDs that turn pixels into electronic signals.

## The Simple Magnifier
- A converging lens with the object inside its focal length gives a magnified virtual image.
- Shorter focal length → more angular magnification (up to about 10×).

> [!equation]
> - **Angular magnification:** $\displaystyle M = \frac{\theta_{\text{image}}}{\theta_{\text{object}}}$

## Microscopes and Telescopes
- In multi-lens systems, each lens's image is the next one's object.
- Overall magnification = objective's linear magnification × eyepiece's angular magnification.
- A telescope's angular magnification is the negative ratio of objective to eyepiece focal lengths.

> [!equation]
> - **Net magnification of a microscope:** $\displaystyle M_{\text{net}} = m^{\text{obj}} M^{\text{eye}} = -\frac{d_i^{\text{obj}}\left(f^{\text{eye}} + 25\ \text{cm}\right)}{f^{\text{obj}} f^{\text{eye}}}$

> [!note] Connection
> Diffraction limits the resolution of these instruments — [[Diffraction]].
