# The Nature of Light

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 1 — Optics › Chapter 1
> ← [[Electromagnetic Waves]] · Next: [[Geometric Optics and Image Formation]] →

**The Nature of Light** opens the optics unit: how light travels, reflects and bends, and how its wave nature shows up in Huygens's principle and polarization.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Speed of light:** $\displaystyle c \approx 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$
> - **Index of refraction:** $\displaystyle n = \frac{c}{v}$
> - **Law of reflection:** $\displaystyle \theta_r = \theta_i$
> - **Snell's law:** $\displaystyle n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$
> - **Critical angle:** $\displaystyle \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\ \text{for } n_1 > n_2$
> - **Malus's law:** $\displaystyle I = I_0 \cos^2\theta$
> - **Brewster's law:** $\displaystyle \tan\theta_b = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$

## The Propagation of Light
- Light travels in a vacuum at about $3.00\times10^8$ m/s.
- The **index of refraction** is the vacuum speed of light divided by its speed in the material.
- **Geometric optics** treats light as straight rays.

> [!equation]
> - **Speed of light:** $\displaystyle c \approx 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s}$
> - **Index of refraction:** $\displaystyle n = \frac{c}{v}$

> [!note] Connection
> Light is an electromagnetic wave with $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$ — see [[Electromagnetic Waves]].

## The Law of Reflection
- A ray reflects off a smooth surface at the same angle it arrived.
- Mirrors reflect at specific angles; rough surfaces scatter (diffuse) light.

> [!equation]
> - **Law of reflection:** $\displaystyle \theta_r = \theta_i$

## Refraction
- **Refraction** is the bending of light as it passes into a different medium.
- **Snell's law** links the two indices of refraction to the angles of the ray.

> [!equation]
> - **Snell's law:** $\displaystyle n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$

## Total Internal Reflection
- The **critical angle** gives a refraction angle of 90°; beyond it, all light reflects back.
- Used in **fiber optics** (with cladding between fibers); it also makes diamonds sparkle.

> [!equation]
> - **Critical angle:** $\displaystyle \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\ \text{for } n_1 > n_2$

## Dispersion
- **Dispersion** splits white light into its wavelengths.
- Rainbows come from refraction and reflection of sunlight; dispersion can cause problems in optical systems.

## Huygens's Principle
- Every point on a wavefront is a source of wavelets; the new wavefront is tangent to them.
- This explains reflection and refraction, and **diffraction** — waves bending around edges.

> [!note] Connection
> Diffraction is developed in [[Diffraction]].

## Polarization
- Polarization is the direction of the wave's electric field; unpolarized light has random directions.
- A polarizing filter halves unpolarized intensity; after that, intensity follows Malus's law.
- Reflection at **Brewster's angle** fully polarizes light; scattering also polarizes it.

> [!equation]
> - **Malus's law:** $\displaystyle I = I_0 \cos^2\theta$
> - **Brewster's law:** $\displaystyle \tan\theta_b = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$

> [!note] Connection
> Polarization is defined by the electric field of the wave — [[Electromagnetic Waves]].
