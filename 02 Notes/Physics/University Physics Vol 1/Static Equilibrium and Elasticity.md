# Static Equilibrium and Elasticity

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 12
> ← [[Angular Momentum]] · Next: [[Gravitation]] →

**Static Equilibrium and Elasticity** covers bodies that don't move — requiring zero net force *and* zero net torque — and how real materials deform under stress.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **First equilibrium condition:** $\displaystyle \sum_k \vec{F}_k = \vec{0}$
> - **Second equilibrium condition:** $\displaystyle \sum_k \vec{\tau}_k = \vec{0}$
> - **Stress and strain:** $\displaystyle \text{stress} = (\text{elastic modulus}) \times \text{strain}$
> - **Young's modulus:** $\displaystyle Y = \frac{F_\perp}{A}\,\frac{L_0}{\Delta L}$
> - **Bulk modulus:** $\displaystyle B = -\Delta p\,\frac{V_0}{\Delta V}$
> - **Shear modulus:** $\displaystyle S = \frac{F_\parallel}{A}\,\frac{L_0}{\Delta x}$

---

## Conditions for Static Equilibrium
- A body is in equilibrium when it stays at rest or in uniform translational and rotational motion.
- Both conditions must hold at once: zero net external force and zero net external torque.
- Free-body diagrams for extended bodies must show the pivot and lever arms.

> [!equation]
> - **First equilibrium condition:** $\displaystyle \sum_k \vec{F}_k = \vec{0}$
> - **Second equilibrium condition:** $\displaystyle \sum_k \vec{\tau}_k = \vec{0}$

> [!note] Connection
> Force balance is from [[Newton's Laws of Motion]]; torque from [[Fixed-Axis Rotation]].

## Examples of Static Equilibrium
- Identify all forces and their lever arms about a chosen axis, draw the free-body diagram, then write both conditions.
- The choice of frame and pivot doesn't change the answer, only how hard the algebra is.

## Stress, Strain, and Elastic Modulus
- **Stress** (Pa) measures deforming forces; **strain** (dimensionless) measures the deformation.
- For small stresses they're proportional — the constant is the **elastic modulus**.
- Tensile/compressive stress → **Young's modulus**; bulk stress (all sides) → **bulk modulus**; shear stress → **shear modulus**.

> [!equation]
> - **Stress and strain:** $\displaystyle \text{stress} = (\text{elastic modulus}) \times \text{strain}$
> - **Young's modulus:** $\displaystyle Y = \frac{F_\perp}{A}\,\frac{L_0}{\Delta L}$
> - **Bulk modulus:** $\displaystyle B = -\Delta p\,\frac{V_0}{\Delta V}$
> - **Shear modulus:** $\displaystyle S = \frac{F_\parallel}{A}\,\frac{L_0}{\Delta x}$

> [!note] Connection
> $Y$ and $B$ set the speed of sound in solids and fluids — see [[Sound]]. Thermal stress from blocked expansion is in [[Temperature and Heat]].

## Elasticity and Plasticity
- **Elastic** materials return to their shape when stress is removed; below the proportionality limit stress ∝ strain.
- Beyond the elastic limit, deformation is **plastic** (permanent) until the breaking point.
