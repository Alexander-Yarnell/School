# Inductance

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 2]] › Unit 2 — Electricity and Magnetism › Chapter 14
> ← [[Electromagnetic Induction]] · Next: [[Alternating-Current Circuits]] →

**Inductance** is induction applied to circuits: a coil opposes changes in current, stores energy in its magnetic field, and combines with resistors and capacitors to make RL, LC and RLC circuits.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon_2 = -M\frac{dI_1}{dt}$$
> $$\varepsilon = -L\frac{dI}{dt}$$
> $$L_{\text{solenoid}} = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l}$$
> $$U = \tfrac{1}{2}LI^2 \qquad u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$$
> $$\tau_{RL} = \frac{L}{R}$$
> $$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$

---

## Mutual Inductance
A changing current in one coil induces an emf in another:

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon_2 = -M\frac{dI_1}{dt}$$

## Self-Inductance
A coil opposes changes in its own current:

> [!equation]
> $$\varepsilon = -L\frac{dI}{dt}$$

For a solenoid:

> [!equation]
> $$L_{\text{solenoid}} = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Both effects come from Faraday's and Lenz's laws in [[Electromagnetic Induction]]. The solenoid's field is in [[Sources of Magnetic Fields]].

## Energy in a Magnetic Field
> [!equation]
> $$U = \tfrac{1}{2}LI^2 \qquad u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Compare a capacitor's stored energy $U = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$ and $u_E = \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0E^2$ in [[Capacitance]].

## RL Circuits
Current rises or decays exponentially with time constant

> [!equation]
> $$\tau_{RL} = \frac{L}{R}$$

> [!note] Connection
> Compare RC circuits ($\tau = RC$) in [[Direct-Current Circuits]].

## LC Circuits
Energy moves back and forth between the capacitor and the inductor, oscillating at

> [!equation]
> $$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$

This is an electrical analog of a mass on a spring.

> [!note] Connection
> Mass-spring oscillations are covered in [[Oscillations]] (Volume 1). The same frequency is the resonance frequency in [[Alternating-Current Circuits]].

## RLC Circuits
Adding resistance **damps** the oscillation.
