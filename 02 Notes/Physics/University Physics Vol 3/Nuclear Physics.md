# Nuclear Physics

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 3]] › Unit 2 — Modern Physics › Chapter 10
> ← [[Condensed Matter Physics]] · Next: [[Particle Physics and Cosmology]] →

**Nuclear Physics** covers the nucleus: what it is made of, why mass turns into binding energy, how unstable nuclei decay, and how fission and fusion release energy.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Mass number:** $\displaystyle A = Z + N$
> - **Isotope notation:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X$
> - **Nuclear radius:** $\displaystyle r = r_0 A^{1/3}$
> - **Mass defect:** $\displaystyle \Delta m = Zm_p + (A - Z)m_n - m_{\text{nuc}}$
> - **Binding energy:** $\displaystyle E = (\Delta m)c^2$
> - **Binding energy per nucleon:** $\displaystyle BEN = \frac{E_b}{A}$
> - **Decay rate:** $\displaystyle -\frac{dN}{dt} = \lambda N$
> - **Radioactive decay law:** $\displaystyle N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
> - **Decay constant and half-life:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{0.693}{T_{1/2}}$
> - **Activity:** $\displaystyle A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
> - **Alpha decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}X + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$
> - **Beta decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}X + {}^{0}_{-1}e + \bar{\nu}$
> - **Positron emission:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z-1}X + {}^{0}_{+1}e + \nu$
> - **Gamma decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X^{*} \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z}X + \gamma$

## Properties of Nuclei
- Nuclei are protons and neutrons: $Z$ protons, $N$ neutrons, $A$ nucleons.
- **Isotopes** have the same $Z$ but different $N$; atomic mass is the weighted average over isotopes.

> [!equation]
> - **Mass number:** $\displaystyle A = Z + N$
> - **Isotope notation:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X$
> - **Nuclear radius:** $\displaystyle r = r_0 A^{1/3}$

## Nuclear Binding Energy
- The **mass defect** is the mass lost when nucleons bind; times $c^2$ it is the **binding energy**.
- The binding energy per nucleon curve shows why splitting heavy nuclei or joining light ones releases energy.

> [!equation]
> - **Mass defect:** $\displaystyle \Delta m = Zm_p + (A - Z)m_n - m_{\text{nuc}}$
> - **Binding energy:** $\displaystyle E = (\Delta m)c^2$
> - **Binding energy per nucleon:** $\displaystyle BEN = \frac{E_b}{A}$

> [!note] Connection
> $E = mc^2$ comes from [[Relativity]].

## Radioactive Decay
- A larger decay constant $\lambda$ means a shorter half-life.
- The decay law estimates ages — e.g. **carbon-14 dating** compares an artifact's C-14 with living tissue.

> [!equation]
> - **Decay rate:** $\displaystyle -\frac{dN}{dt} = \lambda N$
> - **Radioactive decay law:** $\displaystyle N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
> - **Decay constant and half-life:** $\displaystyle \lambda = \frac{0.693}{T_{1/2}}$
> - **Activity:** $\displaystyle A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}$

> [!note] Connection
> Alpha decay happens by tunneling — [[Quantum Mechanics]]. Compare exponential RC decay in [[Direct-Current Circuits]].

## Nuclear Reactions
- **Alpha** decay emits a He-4 nucleus ($Z-2$, $A-4$); **beta** decay emits an electron or positron; **gamma** decay emits a photon.
- A daughter nucleus may decay again, forming a **decay series**.

> [!equation]
> - **Alpha decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}X + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$
> - **Beta decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}X + {}^{0}_{-1}e + \bar{\nu}$
> - **Positron emission:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z-1}X + {}^{0}_{+1}e + \nu$
> - **Gamma decay:** $\displaystyle ^{A}_{Z}X^{*} \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z}X + \gamma$

## Fission
- In **fission**, products have less total mass than the reactants; the binding energy curve explains the energy released.
- Making new isotopes by nuclear transformation is **breeding** (breeder reactors).

## Nuclear Fusion
- **Fusion** joins light nuclei, releasing energy (the **Q value**).
- It powers the Sun and explains nucleosynthesis and the creation of heavy elements.

## Medical Applications and Biological Effects
- Radiopharmaceuticals locate disease and monitor organ function.
- Ionizing radiation damages cells by disrupting reproduction and function.
- Sources include natural isotopes, cosmic rays, soil, building materials and medical tests; effects are measured in units like the rad.
