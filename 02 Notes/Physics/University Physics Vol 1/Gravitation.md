# Gravitation

> [!info] [[University Physics Vol 1]] › Unit 1 — Mechanics › Chapter 13
> ← [[Static Equilibrium and Elasticity]] · Next: [[Fluid Mechanics]] →

**Gravitation** covers Newton's universal law of gravitation and its consequences — weight, orbits, escape velocity, Kepler's laws and tides — ending with Einstein's view of gravity.

## Key Equations

> [!equation]
> - **Newton's law of gravitation:** $\displaystyle \vec{F}_{12} = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{12}$
> - **Gravity at Earth's surface:** $\displaystyle g = G\frac{M_E}{r^2}$
> - **Gravitational PE:** $\displaystyle U = -\frac{GM_E m}{r}$
> - **Conservation of energy:** $\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2}mv_1^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_1} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_2^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_2}$
> - **Escape velocity:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}$
> - **Orbital speed:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{orbit}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}}$
> - **Orbital period:** $\displaystyle T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM_E}}$
> - **Energy in circular orbit:** $\displaystyle E = K + U = -\frac{GmM_E}{2r}$
> - **Conic sections:** $\displaystyle \frac{\alpha}{r} = 1 + e\cos\theta$
> - **Kepler's third law:** $\displaystyle T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$
> - **Schwarzschild radius:** $\displaystyle R_S = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$

## Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation
- Any two masses attract with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
- A spherically symmetric mass acts as if all its mass were at its center.

> [!equation]
> - **Newton's law of gravitation:** $\displaystyle \vec{F}_{12} = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{12}$

> [!note] Connection
> Coulomb's law has the same inverse-square form — see [[Electric Charges and Fields]].

## Gravitation Near Earth's Surface
- Weight is Earth's gravitational attraction on an object.
- Apparent weight differs from true weight when the object is accelerating.

> [!equation]
> - **Gravity at Earth's surface:** $\displaystyle g = G\frac{M_E}{r^2}$

> [!note] Connection
> This is the $g$ used in [[Motion Along a Straight Line]] and [[Newton's Laws of Motion]].

## Gravitational Potential Energy and Total Energy
- Far from Earth, PE must account for gravity weakening with distance.
- Total energy (KE + PE) is constant in orbital motion.
- **Escape velocity** is the minimum speed to leave without returning. Negative total energy means bound; zero or positive means unbound.

> [!equation]
> - **Gravitational PE:** $\displaystyle U = -\frac{GM_E m}{r}$
> - **Conservation of energy:** $\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2}mv_1^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_1} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_2^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_2}$
> - **Escape velocity:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}$

> [!note] Connection
> Compare $U = mgy$ near the surface in [[Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy]].

## Satellite Orbits and Energy
- Orbital speed and period depend on the mass being orbited and the radius — not on the satellite's mass.

> [!equation]
> - **Orbital speed:** $\displaystyle v_{\text{orbit}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}}$
> - **Orbital period:** $\displaystyle T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM_E}}$
> - **Energy in circular orbit:** $\displaystyle E = K + U = -\frac{GmM_E}{2r}$

> [!note] Connection
> Gravity provides the centripetal force — [[Applications of Newton's Laws]].

## Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion
- All orbits are conic sections: bound orbits are circles or ellipses; unbound are parabolas or hyperbolas.
- Constant **areal velocity** reflects conservation of angular momentum.
- The period squared is proportional to the semi-major axis cubed.

> [!equation]
> - **Conic sections:** $\displaystyle \frac{\alpha}{r} = 1 + e\cos\theta$
> - **Kepler's third law:** $\displaystyle T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$

> [!note] Connection
> See [[Angular Momentum]].

## Tidal Forces
- Tides come from differences in the Moon's and Sun's pull across Earth.
- **Spring tides** when Earth, Moon and Sun align; **neap tides** when they form a right angle.

## Einstein's Theory of Gravity
- General relativity explains gravity as the distortion of space-time by mass and energy.
- The **equivalence principle**: mass and acceleration distort space-time indistinguishably.
- Black holes have an event horizon whose radius is proportional to mass.

> [!equation]
> - **Schwarzschild radius:** $\displaystyle R_S = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$

> [!note] Connection
> Special relativity is covered in [[Relativity]].
